Apparatus

Sources & method

A site that spends seven chapters telling you not to trust unsourced claims owes you its own workings. Here they are, including the parts that are weaker than one would like.

How claims are graded

Every substantive statement on this site sits in one of four bands. The bands are about evidence, not about how plausible something feels.

Evidence grading bands used across the archive
BandRequires
Documented Court records, official documents contemporaneous with the event, physical evidence, or multiple independent primary sources in agreement.
Probable Strong circumstantial evidence and/or several independent sources, but no conviction, body, or definitive document. Most of the Chicago disappearances sit here.
Disputed Credible researchers actively disagree, or the fact is established but its interpretation is not — as with the building's blind rooms and shaft.
Invention Traceable to a specific piece of sensational reporting, pulp writing, or self-serving confession, with no independent support.

Three rules applied throughout

  • Holmes is not a source about Holmes. His memoir and confessions are quoted as artefacts of his behaviour, never as evidence of events.
  • 1895 newspapers are evidence of 1895 newspapers. They are indispensable for reconstructing what was said and when the legend formed, and unreliable for what was in the basement.
  • Absence of a body is not absence of a crime — but it does cap a claim at "probable", which is why five of the nine names in the register carry that mark rather than a higher one.

Limitations of this archive

This is a secondary synthesis written for general readers, not original archival research. It has not consulted the Philadelphia court files, Chicago building permits, or Fidelity Mutual's records directly; it relies on published scholarship that has. Dates for undocumented disappearances are approximate by necessity. The floor plans are interpretive reconstructions — no complete original drawing of the building is known to survive, and every published plan, including the famous 1895 newspaper diagrams, is somebody's interpretation.

Where this site says "probably" or "appears to", that is the honest state of the evidence and not hedging for style.

Bibliography

Sources, with their weaknesses stated

Roughly in order of evidentiary weight, not alphabetically.

  1. Adam Selzer, H. H. Holmes: The True History of the White City Devil (Skyhorse, 2017) Documentary research

    The single most important corrective work on the case. Built from Chicago building and licensing records, city directories, court filings, and a systematic reading of the 1890s press against itself. It is the basis for most of the myth-check verdicts on this site, including the finding that the "World's Fair Hotel" cannot be shown to have operated.
    Weakness: a revisionist project by design, and occasionally more confident in its negatives than the surviving record strictly allows.

  2. Frank P. Geyer, The Holmes-Pitezel Case: A History of the Greatest Crime of the Century (Philadelphia, 1896) Primary · public domain

    The investigating detective's own account of the search for the Pitezel children, written immediately afterwards. Plain, procedural, dated, and specific about places and witnesses. Everything this site says about Cincinnati, Indianapolis, Detroit, Toronto and Irvington rests on it.
    Weakness: written by a participant, for a public audience, shortly after the events, and not neutral about its subject.

  3. Trial record, Commonwealth of Pennsylvania v. Herman W. Mudgett, alias H. H. Holmes, Court of Oyer and Terminer, Philadelphia County, October–November 1895 Primary · court record

    The only judicial determination ever made about any of Holmes's killings. Published transcripts and contemporaneous digests supply the medical evidence on the chloroform, the identification testimony, and the conduct of the defence.
    Weakness: its scope is one murder. It is silent on Chicago, which is precisely why so much about Chicago is unresolved.

  4. H. H. Holmes, Holmes' Own Story (1895) and the confession published in the Hearst press, April 1896 Primary · unreliable

    Both written for money, both self-serving, mutually contradictory, and repudiated by their own author on the scaffold. Several people named as victims were subsequently found alive.
    Use: evidence of how Holmes presented himself and of how the inflated body counts entered circulation. Not evidence of events.

  5. Contemporaneous newspaper coverage: Chicago Tribune, Chicago Times-Herald, Chicago Inter Ocean, Philadelphia Inquirer, New York World, July 1895 – May 1896 Primary · sensational

    Indispensable for dating the formation of the legend almost hour by hour, and for showing features appearing in one edition that were absent from the previous day's account of the same room.
    Weakness: circulation-driven competition with minimal verification. This is the origin point of most of the enduring myths.

  6. Erik Larson, The Devil in the White City (Crown, 2003) Narrative history

    The book responsible for the modern shape of the story, interleaving Holmes with Daniel Burnham and the 1893 exposition. Carefully sourced and openly referenced, and a genuinely superb piece of writing.
    Weakness: it draws heavily on the surviving 1890s press record, which carries that record's distortions forward; and its structural pairing of the fair with the killer is a literary device that readers have widely absorbed as a documented connection.

  7. Harold Schechter, Depraved: The Definitive True Story of H. H. Holmes (1994; rev. 2004) Secondary

    The standard full-length treatment before the 2017 revisions, thorough on the Pitezel case and the trial.
    Weakness: largely pre-dates the systematic records work, and accepts several Castle claims that have since been substantially undercut.

  8. Court-authorised exhumation and forensic identification, Holy Cross Cemetery, Yeadon, Pennsylvania, 2017 (University of Pennsylvania forensic anthropology team) Physical evidence

    Undertaken at the request of Holmes's descendants. The coffin was cut out of solid cement; dental comparison identified the remains as Holmes, closing the "he escaped the gallows" theory.

  9. Civil records: U.S. Federal Census 1870–1900; Michigan and New Hampshire vital records; Cook County property and licensing records; Chicago city directories Primary · public

    The ordinary paperwork that decides most of the disputed points — including Elizabeth Holton's survival, the marriage dates, the medical degree, and the absence of a hotel licence in operation.

✦

Corrections

If something here is wrong, it should be fixed rather than defended. This is a static site kept in a public repository; corrections belong in the repository's issue tracker, with a source attached. Claims that arrive without a source will be held to the same standard as the ones this site spends seven chapters rejecting.

On tone

This archive deliberately avoids the aesthetic that usually attends this material — the leering reconstructions, the invented interior monologue, the murderer as antihero. Four of the nine people listed here were children. The design is dark because the subject is; it is not dark because the subject is entertaining.
